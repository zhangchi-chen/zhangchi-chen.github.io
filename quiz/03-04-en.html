To prove that lim(x→0) sin(1/x) does not exist, which pair of probes works?

A. xₙ = 1/(2nπ + π/2) and xₙ = 1/(2nπ)B. xₙ = 1/n and xₙ = 2/nC. xₙ = 1/(2nπ) and xₙ = 1/(4nπ)

A. The first makes sin(1/xₙ) = 1 → 1, the second gives 0 → 0: the two reports differ, so no limit exists.

For the Dirichlet function, at any point x₀, which team of witnesses overturns the claim of a limit?

A. two irrational sequences tending to x₀B. one rational and one irrational sequence, both tending to x₀C. the constant sequence xₙ = x₀

B. Rational probes report 1, irrational ones report 0; the constant sequence is inadmissible because xₙ ≠ x₀ is required.

f(x) = 1 for x > 0 and f(x) = −1 for x < 0. Which pair of witnesses shows it has no limit at 0?

A. xₙ = 1/n and xₙ = 1/n²B. xₙ = −1/n and xₙ = −1/n²C. xₙ = 1/n and xₙ = −1/n

C. The right-hand probes report 1, the left-hand ones −1; differing limits mean no limit.

You checked one sequence xₙ → x₀ and found f(xₙ) → A. What can you conclude?

A. lim(x→x₀) f(x) = AB. nothing yet: other sequences may report differentlyC. the limit exists, but possibly differs from A

B. Proving a limit requires every sequence; the Dirichlet function passes all irrational probes yet still has no limit.

The probe xₙ = n reports eⁿ → +∞. What does it contribute to lim(x→+∞) eˣ?

A. It rules out every finite limit; the full conclusion needs all probes (guaranteed by monotonicity)B. By itself it proves the full conclusionC. It shows the limit is 0

A. One unbounded report vetoes all finite candidates, but confirming +∞ still requires every sequence tending to +∞.
