Which chain is the correct growth ladder (k ≥ 1 fixed, a > 1)?

A. n! ≪ aⁿ ≪ nᵏ ≪ nⁿB. ln n ≪ n ≪ nᵏ ≪ aⁿ ≪ n! ≪ nⁿC. ln n ≪ aⁿ ≪ nᵏ ≪ n! ≪ nⁿ

B. Log, power, exponential, factorial, nⁿ in order; each consecutive ratio tends to 0.

In proving ln n ≪ n, which inequality “pays” for each increment of ln?

A. ln(1 + x) ≤ xB. the binomial theoremC. the AM–GM inequality

A. ln(1 + 1/k) ≤ 1/k ≤ 1, so ln(n+1) = Σ ln(1 + 1/k) ≤ n; applying the same tool to √n gives ln n ≤ 2√n.

Why does keeping a single binomial term already beat nᵏ?

A. because C(n, k+1) = 2ⁿB. because all terms of (1+b)ⁿ are positive and C(n, k+1)bᵏ⁺¹ grows like a constant times nᵏ⁺¹C. because b must exceed 1

B. Dropping positive terms only shrinks the sum, and the kept term is ≥ c·nᵏ⁺¹ — one extra factor of n over nᵏ.

In aⁿ/n! = (a/1)(a/2)⋯(a/n), from where on is every new factor below ½?

A. from n = 1B. after n > aC. after n > 2a

C. a/n < ½ exactly when n > 2a; afterwards the product at least halves at each step and tends to 0.

Comparing 1.01ⁿ with n¹⁰⁰, which statement is right?

A. 1.01 is too close to 1; n¹⁰⁰ stays larger foreverB. from n = 100 on, 1.01ⁿ is largerC. 1.01ⁿ eventually wins, but only after n ≈ 10⁵

C. With a = 1.01 > 1 and k = 100 fixed, the exponential wins in the end; the crossover is near n ≈ 1.17×10⁵, where both sides have 507 digits.

Which choice breaks the rung nᵏ ≪ aⁿ?

A. a = 1.0001B. k = 50C. a = 1

C. With a = 1 the “exponential” is the constant 1; any a > 1, however close to 1, eventually beats a fixed k.
