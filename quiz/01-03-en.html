Which bounded set of rationals has no supremum in ℚ?

A. {x ∈ ℚ : x ≤ 2}B. {x ∈ ℚ : x² < 2}C. {x ∈ ℚ : 0 < x < 1}

B. Its least upper bound would be √2, which is not rational; the suprema 2 and 1 of the other two lie in ℚ.

Which promise belongs to completeness rather than density?

A. Every window (a, b) with a < b contains a rational.B. Nested closed intervals of length → 0 close in on a real number.C. Between two rationals there is another rational.

B. A and C are promises of density (ℚ satisfies them yet has holes); only B says every legitimate hunt ends.

If q > 0 is rational and q² > 2, then for S = {x ∈ ℚ : x² < 2}:

A. q is the least upper bound of S.B. some smaller rational is still an upper bound of S.C. q belongs to S.

B. The upper hunter can always shrink a little and remain an upper bound, so ℚ has no least upper bound here.

The “seed” this course accepts is:

A. Every infinite decimal is a real number, and every real number is an infinite decimal.B. Every real number is rational.C. Every rational Cauchy sequence has a rational limit.

A. The seed characterizes ℝ by infinite decimals; it is itself a completeness statement in disguise.

Which of these is NOT among the five formulations of completeness listed in this episode?

A. Every monotone bounded sequence converges.B. Every bounded sequence is monotone.C. Every Cauchy sequence converges.

B. That statement is simply false (e.g. (−1)ⁿ); the five are Dedekind cuts, nested intervals, monotone convergence, Cauchy sequences, and the supremum principle.

Bisecting f(x) = x² − 2 on [1, 2]: what does completeness guarantee?

A. The algorithm stops after finitely many steps.B. The nested intervals close in on a genuine real number √2.C. The limit of the midpoints is rational.

B. The hunt never stops, but its endpoint is a point of ℝ: the zero √2 exists, and bisection computes its digits.
