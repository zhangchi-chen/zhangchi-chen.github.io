1. 1. Let f ≥ 0 on the first quadrant D, and let the integrals over the quarter disks of radius R tend to L as R → +∞. As a → +∞, the integrals over the squares [0,a]² tend to
  1. Aa limit that may differ from L
  2. BL
  3. C4L/π
    4. Correct: B. Each square lies between the quarter disks of radii a and √2·a; since f ≥ 0, its integral is squeezed to the same L.
2. 2. For a = 3, the squeeze reads (π/4)(1 − e−9) < (∫03 e−x² dx)² < ?
  1. A(π/4)(1 − e−18)
  2. B(π/4)(1 − e−36)
  3. C(π/4)(1 − e−6)
    4. Correct: A. The corner (3, 3) lies at distance 3√2 from the origin, so the outer quarter disk has R² = 18.
3. 3. After the substitution t = √2·u, the integral ∫−∞+∞ e−t²/2 dt equals
  1. A√π/2
  2. B√(2π)
  3. C2π
    4. Correct: B. dt = √2 du and t²/2 = u², so the integral is √2·√π = √(2π).
4. 4. On the unbounded region x² + y² ≥ 1, which improper double integral converges?
  1. A∬ dσ/(x² + y²)
  2. B∬ dσ/(x² + y²)3/2
  3. C∬ dσ/√(x² + y²)
    4. Correct: B. The integrand is r⁻³ with p = 3 > 2: in polar coordinates 2π∫₁ᴿ r⁻² dr = 2π(1 − 1/R) → 2π. A gives 2π ln R and C gives 2π(R − 1), both divergent.
5. 5. On the first quadrant D, Example 1 gives ∬D e−(x²+y²) dσ = π/4. Then ∫0+∞ e−x² dx = ?
  1. Aπ/4
  2. B√π/2
  3. Cπ/2
    4. Correct: B. π/4 is the quadrant integral, which equals the square of the one-variable integral; the one-variable integral itself is √π/2.
