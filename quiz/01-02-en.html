True or false: if S is dense in ℝ, then S has no gaps.

A. TrueB. FalseC. True only when S is countable

B. ℚ is dense, yet √2 ∉ ℚ: dense does not mean filling the line (dense ≠ complete).

When 1/n < b − a, why must some fraction m/n land in (a, b)?

A. The tick spacing is narrower than the window; it cannot jump overB. Because a and b must be rationalC. Because ℚ is complete

A. The first tick to the right of a is at most a + 1/n, and a + 1/n is still less than b.

If r ≠ 0 is rational and r ∈ (a/√2, b/√2), then √2·r:

A. is rational and lies in (a, b)B. is irrational and lies in (a, b)C. is irrational but possibly outside (a, b)

B. Multiplying by √2 maps the window back to (a, b); if √2·r = q ∈ ℚ, then √2 = q/r ∈ ℚ — contradiction.

How many rationals lie between two distinct reals a < b?

A. exactly oneB. finitely manyC. infinitely many

C. After finding one, shrink the window to exclude it and find another — forever.

Which promise belongs to completeness rather than density?

A. Every window gets hitB. Every legitimate hunt ends at a targetC. Every interval contains an irrational

B. Density is about windows; completeness is about whether limits truly exist.
