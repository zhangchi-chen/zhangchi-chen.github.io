Gauss's formula: flux, divergence, and the sources inside

← Notes · Index
1. 1. Take the cube [0,a]³ on its outer side. What is the outward flux of the field below? A = (y(x−z), x², y² + xz)
  1. Aa³
  2. B2a⁴
  3. Ca⁴
    4. Correct: C. The divergence is tiny, and by symmetry x and y each contribute a⁴/2 over the cube. div A = y + 0 + x, ∯_S … = ∭_V (x + y) dV = a⁴/2 + a⁴/2 = a⁴
2. 2. For every small sphere S around a point M₀, enclosing volume ΔV, the outward flux is as below. What happens at M₀? ∯_S P dydz + Q dzdx + R dxdy = −5 ΔV
  1. Adiv A(M₀) = −5, a sink
  2. Bdiv A(M₀) = 5, a source
  3. CIt cannot be decided without choosing coordinates
    4. Correct: A. Divide by the volume and let the sphere shrink to M₀: the limit is the divergence, and it is negative, so M₀ is a sink. div A(M₀) = lim (1/ΔV) ∯_S P dydz + Q dzdx + R dxdy = −5 < 0
3. 3. Σ is the upper hemisphere of radius a, taken on the upper side. What is the flux of the field below through Σ? A = (x, y, z), Σ: x² + y² + z² = a², z ≥ 0
  1. A4πa³
  2. B2πa³
  3. C0
    4. Correct: B. The hemisphere is open, so first close it with a flat lid on its lower side; Gauss gives three times the half-ball's volume, and the lid carries nothing. ∯_{Σ+Σ₀} = 3 · (2/3)πa³ = 2πa³, ∬_{Σ₀} = 0, ∬_Σ = 2πa³
4. 4. S is the unit sphere taken on its inner side. What is the flux of the field below through S? A = (x, y, z)
  1. A4π
  2. B0
  3. C−4π
    4. Correct: C. Gauss gives the outer-side flux, three times the ball's volume; taking the inner side flips its sign. ∯_{−S} = −∯_S = −3 · (4/3)π = −4π
5. 5. The solid V is bounded by the closed surface S, taken on the outer side. Which formula gives its volume ΔV?
  1. AΔV = (1/3) ∯_S x dydz + y dzdx + z dxdy
  2. BΔV = (1/2) ∯_S x dydz + y dzdx + z dxdy
  3. CΔV = ∯_S x dydz + y dzdx + z dxdy
    4. Correct: A. The position field has divergence 3, so the surface integral is three times the volume; the one half belongs to the plane area formula. ∂x/∂x + ∂y/∂y + ∂z/∂z = 3, ∯_S x dydz + y dzdx + z dxdy = 3ΔV
