arctan(tan π) = ?

A. πB. 0C. undefined

B. tan π = 0 and arctan 0 = 0; π lies outside (−π/2, π/2), so the answer is its representative 0.

arcsin(sin 2π/3) = ?

A. 2π/3B. −π/3C. π/3

C. sin 2π/3 = √3/2; the unique x in [−π/2, π/2] with sin x = √3/2 is π/3.

arccos(cos 7π/6) = ?

A. 7π/6B. 5π/6C. π/6

B. cos 7π/6 = −√3/2; the unique x in [0, π] with cos x = −√3/2 is 5π/6.

arctan(tan x) = x holds exactly when

A. x ∈ ℝB. x ∈ [−π/2, π/2]C. x ∈ (−π/2, π/2)

C. tan is undefined at ±π/2, so the identity holds exactly on the open principal interval (−π/2, π/2).

Why is the principal interval of arctan open while that of arcsin is closed?

A. It is merely a conventionB. tan is undefined at ±π/2, while sin attains ±1 thereC. Because ℝ is an open set

B. open or closed is dictated by the original function: sin reaches its endpoint values, while ±π/2 are not even in the domain of tan.

sin(arcsin x) = x holds for

A. every x ∈ ℝB. every x ∈ [−1, 1]C. every x ∈ [−π/2, π/2]

B. arcsin is defined only on [−1, 1]; outside it the composition does not even exist.
