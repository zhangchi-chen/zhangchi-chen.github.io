To compare aₙ = 1 + (−1)ⁿ/n with bₙ = 1, why does “comparing sizes” fail?

A. Every term of aₙ is greater than 1.B. Term by term, the inequality flips back and forth.C. aₙ has no limit.

B. a₁ < 1, a₂ > 1, a₃ < 1 … so instead one compares the distance |aₙ − 1| = 1/n and the long-run trend.

“x near x₀ forces f(x) near f(x₀)” names which concept?

A. ContinuityB. The derivativeC. Completeness

A. Continuity is the hope that limits pass through functions: f(lim xₙ) = lim f(xₙ).

f(t) = 100 + t versus g(t) = t²: in the long run, who leads is decided by…

A. their values at t = 0.B. which formula is shorter.C. their growth rates, i.e. the derivatives.

C. f′(t) = 1 while g′(t) = 2t keeps growing, so g overtakes permanently around t ≈ 10.5.

What does a Taylor expansion do?

A. Approximates good functions locally by polynomials, with a remainder.B. Proves that every sequence converges.C. Finds a function's maximum.

A. For example near 0, sin x ≈ x − x³/6 + x⁵/120; controlling the remainder is the real skill.

Why does the sequence 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, … need the completeness of the reals?

A. Every term is irrational.B. Its limit √2 is not rational and has nowhere to land in ℚ.C. It diverges to ∞.

B. Completeness guarantees that this limit exists as a real number — ℝ has no holes.

For aₙ = (−1)ⁿ(1 + 1/n), the upper limit is

A. 0B. 2C. 1

C. The even terms 1 + 1/n tend to 1 from above and the odd terms tend to −1, so limsup = 1 and liminf = −1.
