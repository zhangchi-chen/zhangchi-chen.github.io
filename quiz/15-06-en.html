1. 1. Which production line puts ζ(4) = π⁴/90 on the wall?
  1. Athe sine series of x(π − x) at x = π/2
  2. Bthe Parseval equality on the coefficients of x²
  3. Cthe sawtooth at x = 2π/3
    4. Correct: B. squaring aₙ = 4(−1)ⁿ/n² gives 16/n⁴ with every sign plus, so 2π⁴/5 = 2π⁴/9 + 16ζ(4).
2. 2. In the Parseval equality for f = x² on [−π, π], the constant term contributes:
  1. Aπ⁴/9
  2. B2π⁴/9
  3. C2π⁴/5
    4. Correct: B. a₀ = 2π²/3, and the equality uses a₀²/2 = 2π⁴/9; π⁴/9 is (a₀/2)², and 2π⁴/5 is the left side.
3. 3. β(3) = π³/32 comes from:
  1. Aa cosine series of an even polynomial at x = 0
  2. Bthe sine series of the odd extension of x(π − x) at x = π/2
  3. Cthe Parseval equality on |x|
    4. Correct: B. odd powers of n come only with sines; at π/2 the odd sines alternate ±1. A and C give only even powers.
4. 4. Where does the sawtooth Σ sin nx/n = (π − x)/2 make its sines follow χ₃, up to a factor?
  1. Ax = π/2
  2. Bx = 2π/3
  3. Cx = π/3
    4. Correct: B. sin(2nπ/3) = (√3/2)·χ₃(n); x = π/2 gives χ₄, and x = π/3 gives a period-6 pattern.
5. 5. L(1, χ₃) = 1 − 1/2 + 1/4 − 1/5 + ⋯ equals:
  1. Aπ/(3√3)
  2. Bπ/6
  3. Cπ/4
    4. Correct: A. at x = 2π/3 the sawtooth is π/6 = (√3/2)·L(1, χ₃); π/6 forgets the factor, π/4 is β(1).
6. 6. “ζ(3) can be read off the cosine series of some polynomial on [0, π] at a clever point; nobody has found the point yet.”
  1. Atrue
  2. Bfalse: those cosine series carry only even powers of n, at every point
  3. Ctrue, at x = 0
    4. Correct: B. parity bookkeeping: cosine coefficients of such functions involve 1/n², 1/n⁴, …, never 1/n³; the odd powers live on the sine line, whose signs are never all plus.
