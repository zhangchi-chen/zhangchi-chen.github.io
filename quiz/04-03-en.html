The Extreme Value Theorem

Click the button under each item to reveal the answer.

1. Which pairing of roles is correct?

A.Lemma: f is bounded on [a, b]. Theorem: f attains its max and min on [a, b].B.Lemma: f attains its max on [a, b]. Theorem: f is bounded on [a, b].C.Both say the same thing, so one of them is redundant.
Answer: A. Boundedness is the lemma (a ceiling exists); attainment is the theorem (the ceiling is touched). Step two even uses step one.

2. f(x) = x on the open interval (0, 1). What is true?

A.f has maximum 1.B.f is bounded, but has neither a maximum nor a minimum.C.f is unbounded.
Answer: B. 0 < f(x) < 1, so f is bounded; sup = 1 and inf = 0, but no x in (0, 1) gives f(x) = 1 or f(x) = 0. The values escape through the open ends.

3. On [0, 1], let f(x) = x for 0 < x < 1 and f(0) = f(1) = 1/2. What goes wrong?

A.Closedness fails, and the maximum is 1.B.Continuity fails, and sup f = 1 is never reached.C.Continuity fails, but the maximum 1/2 is attained at the endpoints.
Answer: B. The interval is closed; the endpoint jumps break continuity. Every value is below 1 (inside: f(x) = x < 1; endpoints: 1/2 < 1), yet values approach 1, so sup = 1 is never reached. And 1/2 is no maximum: f(0.9) = 0.9 > 1/2.

4. In step one (boundedness), where exactly is closedness of [a, b] used?

A.To produce the points xₙ with f(xₙ) > n.B.To guarantee that the limit c of the subsequence lies in [a, b].C.To guarantee that M = sup f exists.
Answer: B. From a ≤ yₖ ≤ b and yₖ → c we get a ≤ c ≤ b, so f is defined and continuous at c. The points xₙ come from assuming unboundedness; M = sup f belongs to step two and comes from the supremum principle.

5. Suppose f(x) < M everywhere and the helper g(x) = 1/(M − f(x)) satisfies g(x) ≤ K with K > 0. What follows?

A.f(x) ≤ M − 1/K for every x.B.f(x) ≥ M − 1/K for every x.C.f(x) ≤ M − K for every x.
Answer: A. Both sides are positive, so 1/(M − f(x)) ≤ K gives M − f(x) ≥ 1/K, that is f(x) ≤ M − 1/K. That is a smaller upper bound than M, contradicting M = sup f.

6. For f(x) = x on [0, 1), M = 1 and g(x) = 1/(1 − x). What is g(0.99), and what does it tell us?

A.g(0.99) = 100; g is unbounded, and there is no contradiction because [0, 1) is not closed.B.g(0.99) = 99; g stays bounded.C.g(0.99) = 100; this is a contradiction, so f must reach 1.
Answer: A. 1 − 0.99 = 0.01 and 1/0.01 = 100; likewise g(0.999) = 1000. g blows up, exactly as 'never reached' predicts. The lemma needs a closed interval, so here it says nothing — no contradiction, and indeed f never reaches 1.
