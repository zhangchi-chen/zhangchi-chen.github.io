Which of the following is a subsequence of (aₙ)?

A. (a₂, a₂, a₅, …)B. (a₄, a₉, a₁₆, …)C. (a₅, a₃, a₈, …)

B. Only 4 < 9 < 16 is strictly increasing; the other two replay or rewind indices.

For any subsequence (aₙₖ), which inequality always holds?

A. nₖ ≤ kB. nₖ ≥ kC. nₖ = k

B. Among k strictly increasing positive integers, the k-th is at least k.

In the construction, why require nₖ₊₁ > max(n₁, …, nₖ)?

A. convergence is proved at that stepB. merely to keep the indices strictly increasingC. to shrink the band

B. It just keeps the indices in order: the pointer only moves forward, nothing more.

When is aₘ a peak?

A. aₘ ≥ aₙ for all n > mB. aₘ ≥ aₙ for all n < mC. just aₘ > aₘ₊₁

A. A peak is not smaller than any term that comes after it.

If a sequence has only finitely many peaks, what can be built after the last peak?

A. a non-increasing subsequence made of peaksB. an increasing subsequenceC. generally nothing

B. After the last peak, every term is eventually exceeded; hopping forward from each term yields an increasing subsequence.

About aₙ = (−1)ⁿ, which statement is correct?

A. it converges to 0B. it diverges but has convergent subsequencesC. it has no convergent subsequence

B. The even and odd terms form the constant subsequences 1 and −1 respectively.
