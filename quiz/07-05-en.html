In the dictionary, the increment Δaₙ = aₙ₊₁ − aₙ corresponds to…

A. f(x)/g(x)B. f′(x)C. ∫ f′

B. A one-step increment is a discrete derivative; summed increments are a discrete integral.

For bₙ = 2 − 1/n and aₙ = 3 − 1/n, which checklist item fails?

A. strictly increasingB. bₙ → +∞C. the ratio of increments has a limit

B. bₙ increases but stays below 2; the head never washes out, so aₙ/bₙ → 3/2 instead of 1.

In the proof, why do the initial stocks aₘ, bₘ eventually not matter?

A. they are divided away by bₙ → +∞B. one may take aₘ = 0C. because ε → 0

A. The head appears as (aₘ − (L ± ε)bₘ)/bₙ, which tends to 0 since bₙ → +∞.

For each fixed ε, what does the squeeze give directly?

A. lim aₙ/bₙ = L + εB. limsup ≤ L + ε and liminf ≥ L − εC. the increments become monotone

B. Both limsup and liminf are trapped in the ε-neighborhood of L; since ε is arbitrary, they can only meet at L.

lim (1 + 2 + … + n)/n² equals

A. 1B. 1/2C. 0

B. The increments are n + 1 and 2n + 1; their ratio tends to 1/2, and n² is strictly increasing to +∞.

The example aₙ = (−1)ⁿ, bₙ = n shows…

A. the converse of the theorem failsB. bₙ must decreaseC. one must use the 0/0 form

A. aₙ/bₙ → 0, but the ratio of increments 2(−1)ⁿ⁺¹ has no limit: the theorem is one-directional.
