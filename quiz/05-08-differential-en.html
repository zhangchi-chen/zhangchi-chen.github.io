The differential: a linear stand-in, and why the formula survives a change of variable · Quiz

1. 1. For f(x) = x² at x₀ = 3, the differential dy sends the step Δx to:

  1. A. A. 6·Δx
  2. B. B. 6·Δx + (Δx)²
  3. C. C. 9 + 6·Δx
A — dy = f′(3)·Δx = 6Δx; B is the whole increment Δy, and C is the height of the tangent line.

2. 2. Which sentence is Theorem 5.9 (one variable)?

  1. A. A. If f is continuous at x₀, then f is differentiable at x₀.
  2. B. B. f is differentiable at x₀ if and only if f′(x₀) exists, and then A = f′(x₀).
  3. C. C. If f′(x₀) exists, f is differentiable at x₀, but A can differ from f′(x₀).
B — both directions hold, and the constant is forced: A is the limit of Δy/Δx. A fails for |x| at 0.

3. 3. y = sin(x³). Using dy = f′(u) du with u = x³:

  1. A. A. dy = cos(x³) dx
  2. B. B. dy = 3x²·cos(x³) dx
  3. C. C. dy = cos(3x²) dx
B — dy = cos u du and du = 3x² dx; A forgets to peel the inner layer.

4. 4. On the circle x² + y² = 25 at the point (3, 4):

  1. A. A. dy/dx = −3/4, dx/dy = −4/3
  2. B. B. dy/dx = 3/4, dx/dy = 4/3
  3. C. C. dy/dx = −4/3, dx/dy = −3/4
A — 2x dx + 2y dy = 0 gives dy/dx = −x/y = −3/4 and dx/dy = −y/x = −4/3, with no choice of independent variable.

5. 5. On x = t², y = t³, the slope dy/dx at t = 1 is:

  1. A. A. 3/2
  2. B. B. 2/3
  3. C. C. 3
A — dx = 2t dt and dy = 3t² dt, so dy/dx = 3t/2 = 3/2: a true quotient of differentials.

6. 6. Trap: “dy equals Δy.” Check it on f(x) = x² at x₀ = 1 with Δx = 0.1.

  1. A. A. True: dy = Δy = 0.21
  2. B. B. False: dy = 0.2 but Δy = 0.21
  3. C. C. False: dy = 0.21 but Δy = 0.2
B — dy = 2·0.1 = 0.2, Δy = 1.1² − 1 = 0.21; the gap 0.01 is the corner (Δx)². They agree only for linear f.
