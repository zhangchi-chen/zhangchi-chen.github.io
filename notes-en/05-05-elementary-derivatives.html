Motivation

  1. Today the whole derivative table grows out of just three formulas.
  2. Midnight before the quiz, and two formula sheets disagree about aˣ.
  3. One multiplies by ln a, the other divides by ln a.
  4. Memory alone cannot settle it; you need a way to rebuild the row.
  5. Key move: aˣ is the same function as exp(x ln a).
  6. Outer card eᵘ copies itself; the inner u = x ln a contributes ln a.
  7. So (aˣ)′ = aˣ ln a, and your sheet was right.
  8. Check with 2ˣ: at 0 the slope should be ln 2 ≈ 0.69.
  9. The roommate's 1/ln 2 ≈ 1.44 cuts through the curve instead of touching.
  10. Could the whole table be rebuilt like that, from a few seeds?

What the statement says

  1. First, the course text's five rules, exactly as written.
  2. Each rule takes derivatives you already know and outputs a new one.
  3. Then the basic formulas: constants, powers and the four trig rows.
  4. And the inverse trig, exponential and logarithm rows complete the list.
  5. A constant never moves, so its rate of change is zero.
  6. Any real α is allowed; at x = 1 the tangent slope is exactly α.
  7. Slide along sin x and record its slope: the record traces cos x.
  8. (tan x)′ = sec²x is at least 1, so tan climbs on every branch.
  9. Where sin x turns flat at the ends, arcsin x turns steep.
  10. eˣ is its own slope: the higher the curve, the steeper it climbs.
  11. ln|x| has slope 1/x: steep near 0, nearly flat far away.
  12. Fourteen formulas plus five rules, and almost none needs memorizing.

Method / proof core

  1. Picture a reunion photo wall: three grandparent photos at the very top.
  2. Card one: eᵘ, whose derivative with respect to u is itself.
  3. Card two: sin u, whose derivative is cos u.
  4. Card three: the power uᵅ, whose derivative is αuᵅ⁻¹.
  5. Below them, five rules are the arrows that produce every other photo.
  6. 05-04 promised this: chain and inverse turn three cards into the table.
  7. First child, already met: aˣ comes from eᵘ by the chain rule.
  8. Next, ln x is the inverse of eˣ: y = ln x means x = eʸ.
  9. Inverse rule: dy/dx = 1/(dx/dy) = 1/eʸ = 1/x for x > 0.
  10. The negative side agrees, so (ln|x|)′ = 1/x for every x ≠ 0.
  11. Change of base: logₐ|x| is ln|x| times the constant 1/ln a.
  12. Its slope is 1/(x ln a): ln a sits downstairs, unlike in aˣ.
  13. The constant row needs no parent: its difference quotient is always 0.
  14. Trig branch: first write cos x as a composite of sin.
  15. Outer card gives cos(π/2 − x) = sin x; the inner factor is −1.
  16. So (cos x)′ = −sin x, and the photo goes up on the wall.
  17. tan = sin/cos, so the quotient rule takes over.
  18. The numerator is cos²x + sin²x = 1, so (tan x)′ = sec²x.
  19. cot = cos/sin goes the same way to −csc²x; both join the wall.
  20. For arcsin, read it backwards: x = sin y with |y| < π/2.
  21. Inverse rule: 1/cos y, and cos y = √(1 − x²) on that range.
  22. arcsin joins the wall; arccos = π/2 − arcsin follows by the sum rule.
  23. arctan: invert tan; since sec²y = 1 + x², the slope is 1/(1 + x²).
  24. arctan and arccot arrive, and the whole trig family is rebuilt.
  25. Power branch: (1 + x²)⁻¹ is the card uᵅ with u = 1 + x², α = −1.
  26. Card, then inner slope: −(1 + x²)⁻² · 2x = −2x/(1 + x²)².
  27. Any exponent n works the same way, in one motion.
  28. Three cards, five rules: every row of the course list is now on the wall.
  29. Now the reverse job: a nested function arrives. Where do you start?
  30. Like a prerequisite chart: read the top box before signing up below.
  31. The rule: name the outermost operation before touching the pen.
  32. First example: outer card eᵘ, inner layer u = −x².
  33. Card times inner slope: exp(−x²)·(−2x).
  34. Forget the −2x, and at x = 1 even the sign of the slope is wrong.
  35. Second example, sin(2x + 1): outer sin, inner slope 2.
  36. Forget the 2, and your tangent at 0 is only half as steep.
  37. Third example, (x² + 1)¹⁰: outer u¹⁰, inner x² + 1.
  38. Power card times 2x: the result is 20x(x² + 1)⁹.
  39. Expanding first would mean eleven terms; outermost-first skips all that.
  40. Careful: rules only work on open pieces; break points need the definition.
  41. Find a and b so that x² and ax + b join smoothly at x = 1.
  42. Tempting shortcut: differentiate both pieces and set 2x = a at 1.
  43. That gives a = 2, and says nothing at all about b.
  44. Take b = 0: slopes match, yet f jumps from 1 to 2 at the join.
  45. A jump breaks continuity, and without continuity there is no derivative.
  46. So go back to the definition: the one-sided quotients from 05-02.
  47. Step one, continuity: the line must arrive at f(1) = 1, so a + b = 1.
  48. Step two, slopes: left quotients tend to 2, right ones to a.
  49. So a = 2 and b = −1: the line is exactly the tangent of x² at 1.
  50. Matching slopes found a; only continuity could ever find b.
  51. A habit that makes this automatic: store every row as a function of u.
  52. Each row ends in ·u′, so table and chain rule fire in one motion.
  53. Example: ln|cos x|. The outer row is ln|u| with u = cos x.
  54. u′/u = −sin x/cos x = −tan x, wherever cos x ≠ 0.
  55. One breath on hyperbolic functions: built from eˣ, sinh′ = cosh and cosh′ = sinh.

Applications and outlook

  1. Use one: the angle the board fills when you sit x metres back.
  2. θ = arctan(2/x): outer arctan u, inner u = 2/x.
  3. Table row and chain in one motion: θ′ = −2/(x² + 4).
  4. At 2 m the angle shrinks 0.25 rad per metre; at 6 m, only 0.05.
  5. Use two: news spreading through a dorm follows σ(t) = 1/(1 + e⁻ᵗ).
  6. Power card with exponent −1, then the chain: σ′ = e⁻ᵗ/(1 + e⁻ᵗ)².
  7. Rewritten, σ′ = σ(1 − σ): spreading is fastest when half the dorm knows.
  8. That is why machine-learning code loves σ: its slope reuses its own value.
  9. 05-04 promised chain plus inverse would build the table: paid in full.
  10. A debt to 06-02: the derivative limit theorem will check some joins via lim f′.
  11. But continuity at the join must still be checked by hand, every time.
  12. Everything elementary now differentiates mechanically, so exams move the difficulty.
  13. Break points, parameters, and proofs about f′ rather than computations of it.
  14. The next two episodes are the first places where that shift happens.
  15. Three cards and five rules: regenerate any row, any time you need it.
