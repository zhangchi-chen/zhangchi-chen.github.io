Motivation

  1. Fermat and Rolle: three conditions, none removable.
  2. Saturday: the hiking club walks a loop trail.
  3. You start and finish at the same lot, same altitude.
  4. Claim: somewhere on the way, the trail was perfectly flat.
  5. A summit, a valley, or the whole path level.
  6. Flat means the tangent is horizontal: slope zero.
  7. In symbols: some point ξ inside has f′(ξ) = 0.
  8. Then your roommate sketches three trips where it fails.
  9. Each trip left one piece of gear home: tent, boots, or map.
  10. So which conditions does the claim really need — and why?

What the statement says

  1. First, the engine underneath: Fermat's lemma.
  2. At an extremum where f is differentiable, f′(x₀) = 0.
  3. Clause one: f is defined on a whole neighbourhood of x₀.
  4. Clause two: f is differentiable at x₀.
  5. Clause three: x₀ is an extremum point, a local max or min.
  6. Then the tangent there is horizontal: f′(x₀) = 0.
  7. Why? Draw chords from the peak x₀ to nearby points.
  8. On the right, chords run downhill: the quotient is ≤ 0.
  9. On the left, chords climb: the quotient is ≥ 0.
  10. Both one-sided limits equal f′(x₀): at most 0, at least 0, so 0.
  11. Recall 05-02: a derivative needs both one-sided derivatives to agree.
  12. At an endpoint only one side exists, so nothing squeezes.
  13. Now Rolle's theorem turns the hike into mathematics.
  14. Three conditions go in; one flat point comes out.
  15. Condition (i): continuous on the closed interval [a, b].
  16. Condition (ii): differentiable on the open interval (a, b).
  17. Endpoints are exempt: a semicircle's vertical ends are fine.
  18. Condition (iii): equal heights at both ends, f(a) = f(b).
  19. Conclusion: some ξ in (a, b) has f′(ξ) = 0.
  20. At least one, maybe several, and it won't say where.
  21. The text warns: drop any one condition and it may fail.

Method / proof core

  1. Proof idea: find a summit or a valley strictly inside.
  2. Step one: continuous on [a, b], f attains a max M and min m.
  3. That's 04-03's extreme value theorem: condition (i) is used here.
  4. Case 1: m = M, f is constant, and any ξ works.
  5. Case 2: m < M, and the trail really goes up and down.
  6. Equal ends: at most one of M, m sits at an endpoint.
  7. So the other lies strictly inside: condition (iii) is used.
  8. Inside, f is differentiable: condition (ii) is used.
  9. Fermat's lemma then gives f′(ξ) = 0 there.
  10. One proof, three conditions, each running its own step.
  11. Now the gear test: leave one condition home, keep the rest.
  12. Wrong map: drop (iii) and take f(x) = x on [0, 1].
  13. Continuous and differentiable, but the end values are 0 and 1.
  14. f′ ≡ 1 everywhere: never zero, no flat spot.
  15. No tent: drop (i). Let f(0) = 1 and f(x) = x for 0 < x ≤ 1.
  16. Both ends equal 1, and f is differentiable inside.
  17. Still f′ ≡ 1: the lowest level 0 is never reached.
  18. No boots: drop (ii) and take f(x) = |x| on [−1, 1].
  19. Continuous, and f(−1) = f(1) = 1: two conditions hold.
  20. The only interior extremum is the corner, where f′ doesn't exist.
  21. Elsewhere f′ = ±1, so Fermat has nowhere to land.
  22. Three trips, three failures, each at the step its condition fed.
  23. Careful: dropping a condition doesn't forbid a flat spot.
  24. The conditions are sufficient, not necessary.
  25. The Rolle pattern on roots: course-text Example 1.
  26. Let f be differentiable on ℝ, with f′(x) = 0 having no real root.
  27. Then f(x) = 0 has at most one real root.
  28. Suppose instead there are two roots x₁ < x₂.
  29. On [x₁, x₂] all three conditions hold, with f(x₁) = f(x₂) = 0.
  30. Rolle hands us ξ with f′(ξ) = 0: contradiction.
  31. The move: build an interval where Rolle applies, then let ξ work.
  32. A name for the flat spots: stationary points.
  33. Fermat: a differentiable extremum point is always stationary.
  34. The reverse fails: look at f(x) = x³ at 0.
  35. The tangent at 0 is flat, but the curve crosses it.
  36. Left of 0, f < 0; right of 0, f > 0: no extremum.
  37. Stationary does not imply extremum. Keep this picture.

Applications and outlook

  1. Use one: how many real roots does x³ + 3x − 1 have?
  2. Its derivative 3x² + 3 is at least 3, never zero.
  3. So, by Example 1, at most one real root.
  4. And f(0) = −1 < 0 < 3 = f(1), so at least one.
  5. Exactly one real root, between 0 and 1.
  6. Use two: between two roots of f lies a root of f′.
  7. Example: p(x) = (x − 1)(x − 2)(x − 3), roots 1, 2, 3.
  8. Rolle on [1, 2] and [2, 3]: p′ has a root in each.
  9. Check: p′ = 3x² − 12x + 11 has roots 2 ± √3/3 ≈ 1.42, 2.58.
  10. n distinct real roots of f force at least n − 1 roots of f′.
  11. Unequal ends? One subtraction levels them; that's 06-02.
  12. That subtraction is our first auxiliary function; 06-11 collects the rest.
  13. The pattern returns: equal ends force a flat spot inside.
  14. With integrals: a running area with equal values at both ends.
  15. In two variables: equal boundary values force a flat tangent plane.
  16. Two equal numbers in a problem? Look between them for a flat spot.
  17. Three conditions, three ways to fail: none removable.
