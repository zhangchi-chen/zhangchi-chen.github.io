Cantor's Theorem

Hello, everyone.Today we hunt for one ruler that serves a whole interval.Last time, x² on [0, ∞) had a runaway δ.Uniform continuity demands one δ for every pair of points.Pointwise continuity lets δ change from point to point.

Now picture [a, b] as a walled courtyard.Every point x carries its own local δ(x).Tempting idea: collect them all and take the smallest.The walls keep everything finite, right?Pause and ask: how many points live in the courtyard?Infinitely many, so infinitely many δ's.Look at 1, 1/2, 1/3, …: every one is positive.

Yet their infimum is 0.Infinitely many numbers need not have a minimum.And δ = 0 is no ruler at all.Let's stress-test the idea on (0, 1] with f(x) = 1/x.The same story goes through word for word.But 1/x is not uniformly continuous there.Take neighbours 1/n and 1/(n+1).

Their distance shrinks to 0, yet the values stay 1 apart.So the intuition never used the walls!It points somewhere true, but it is not a proof.Rigor must make the walls do real work.Cantor: continuous on [a, b] means uniformly continuous on [a, b].Proof by contradiction: suppose the conclusion fails.Then a hostile ε₀ defeats every δ.

Taking δ = 1/n, we get a bad pair (xₙ, yₙ).The points close in, yet their values stay ≥ ε₀ apart.Three beats will turn this into a contradiction.Beat one: the sequence xₙ is bounded.Bolzano–Weierstrass extracts xₙₖ → x₀.Question: where does x₀ live?Passing to the limit: a ≤ x₀ ≤ b.

The interval is closed, so x₀ stays inside.Here the wall does its job, legally.On (0, 1], the limit could escape to 0.Beat two: look at the partners yₙₖ.Each yₙₖ sits within 1/nₖ of its xₙₖ.Triangle inequality: yₙₖ is dragged to x₀.Both sequences meet at one checkpoint, x₀.

Beat three: f is continuous at x₀.So f(xₙₖ) → f(x₀).Likewise f(yₙₖ) → f(x₀).Their gap is squeezed to 0.But every gap was ≥ ε₀: contradiction!So f is uniformly continuous. ∎

Recap: trap, converge, collide.The closed interval compresses infinitely many demands into one checkpoint.Closedness and continuity are both essential.Gluing lemma: uniform on each piece means uniform on the union.Two territories, each with its own ruler δ₁, δ₂.They meet at the border town c.Same side: δ₁ or δ₂ already works.

Cross-border pairs are the danger.The town c sits between x′ and x″.Continuity at c gives δ₃ for ε/2.Each side lands within ε/2 of f(c).Add them: the gap is less than ε.So δ = min{δ₁, δ₂, δ₃} rules the union.Note: the border town belongs to both territories.

Lipschitz condition: a bounded stretching factor L gives uniform continuity.L is a bounded stretching factor.Given ε, take δ = ε/L.Then distance below δ forces the value gap below ε.No limits, no subsequences: one line.Try x²: factor the difference.The stretching factor is exactly |x′ + x″|.

On [0, M] it is at most 2M, so L = 2M.On [0, ∞) it grows without bound.Gap 1/n, yet the values differ by more than 2.That is last episode's runaway δ in new clothes.

Now look at √x, territory by territory.Rationalize the numerator.On [1, ∞) the denominator is at least 2.So L = 1/2 there.Near 0, though, the factor blows up.On [0, 1], √x has no Lipschitz constant.Yet Cantor still makes it uniformly continuous there.

Glue at 1: uniformly continuous on all of [0, ∞).Lipschitz is sufficient, not necessary.Showcase: cos(√x) on the whole half-line.Territory [0, 1]: hand it to Cantor.Territory [1, ∞): find a Lipschitz bound.Sum-to-product first.Then |sin t| ≤ |t| and |sin s| ≤ 1.

So cosine stretches by a factor of at most 1.Chain it with the √x bound.So L = 1/2, and δ = 2ε works.Glue at the seam x = 1.So cos(√x) is uniformly continuous on [0, ∞).No derivatives, only inequalities.The closed interval grants the upgrade for free.

But the whole real line ℝ has no walls.When does a function on ℝ still earn the guarantee?That exam favorite is next: limits at infinity.Today's idea: compressing the infinite into the finite.See you next episode.
