Mathematical Analysis · Episode 0

What is analysis? The art of comparison

Analysis is the craft of extending “compare” — first by size, then by distance, then by trend — using precise mathematics.

0Opening question

Which is bigger: 0.999… or 1? Any gap you name, enough nines undercut it: 1 − 0.99…9 = 10−n. Deciding this needs a way to compare with infinity.

1Compare by size

7 > 3, √2 > 1.4

Two numbers compare instantly. But sequences and functions are not single numbers — put them on a balance and it only wobbles. “Compare” must be re-invented.

2Compare by distance and trend → limit

|an − 1| = 1/n,  an → 1

For an = 1 + (−1)n/n and bn = 1 the order flips forever. So measure the distance to a target and watch the long run, ignoring any finite beginning. That is the limit of a sequence — and with it, approximation.

3Let the limit pass through → continuity

f(lim xn) = lim f(xn)

If x only approximates x0, we want f(x) to approximate f(x0): 2.99² = 8.9401, 2.999² = 8.994001. Wanting the limit to pass through the function is continuity. A jump breaks it.

4Compare growth speed → derivative

f′,  xn+1 = xn − f(xn)/f′(xn)

f(t) = 100 + t starts ahead, g(t) = t² grows faster and overtakes near t ≈ 10.5. Current size does not settle the race; the growth rate — the derivative — does. The tangent line gives linear approximation, and Newton’s method turns it into fast root-finding: the correct digits of √2 roughly double each step.

5Fit better and better → Taylor expansion

sin x ≈ x − x³/6 + x⁵/120

One tangent fits only near one point. Higher derivatives give better polynomial fits: the Taylor expansion, with a remainder term to control. Bending also gives convexity and inequalities such as eˣ ≥ 1 + x for every real x.

6The ground beneath → completeness

0.999… = 1

Every “it converges” assumes the limit exists. 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, … aims at √2, which is not rational: in ℚ there is a hole. The real numbers have none — completeness, with many equivalent faces proved equivalent later. For sequences that never converge, the limit superior and limit inferior still describe the long run: for (−1)n(1 + 1/n) they are 1 and −1.

7What you will learn to do

  1. Read and write precise definitions (the ε–N language arrives next episodes).
  2. Verify a definition point by point when writing a proof.
  3. Estimate and compare — the everyday craft of inequalities.
  4. Build small counterexamples that break false claims.
  5. Draw the picture before writing the formula.

Self-check

Pick an answer in your head, then reveal.

1. Why can’t we compare an = 1 + (−1)n/n with bn = 1 by size?
A. Every term of an is larger than 1.B. The order flips back and forth from term to term.C. The sequence an has no limit.
Correct: B. a1 < 1, a2 > 1, a3 < 1, … — so we compare distance |an − 1| = 1/n and the long-run trend instead.
2. Which idea says “x near x0 gives f(x) near f(x0)”?
A. ContinuityB. DerivativeC. Completeness
Correct: A. Continuity is the wish that the limit passes through the function: f(lim xn) = lim f(xn).
3. f(t) = 100 + t versus g(t) = t²: what decides who leads in the long run?
A. Their values at t = 0.B. Which formula is shorter.C. Their growth rates, i.e. derivatives.
Correct: C. f′(t) = 1 while g′(t) = 2t keeps climbing, so g overtakes near t ≈ 10.5 for good.
4. What does a Taylor expansion do?
A. Approximates a good function locally by a polynomial, with a remainder term.B. Proves that every sequence converges.C. Finds the largest value of a function.
Correct: A. For example sin x ≈ x − x³/6 + x⁵/120 near 0; controlling the remainder is the real work.
5. Why does the sequence 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, … need the completeness of the real numbers?
A. Its terms are irrational.B. Its limit √2 is not rational, so in ℚ it has nowhere to land.C. It diverges to ∞.
Correct: B. Completeness guarantees that such limits exist as real numbers — ℝ has no holes.
6. For an = (−1)n(1 + 1/n), what is the limit superior?
A. 0B. 2C. 1
Correct: C. The even terms 1 + 1/n approach 1 from above and the odd terms approach −1, so lim sup = 1 and lim inf = −1.
