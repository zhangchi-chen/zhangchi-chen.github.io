19-03 The Gamma and Beta functions: the sequel to the factorial

The Gamma and Beta functions: the sequel to the factorial

1 · Motivation

The Gamma and Beta functions, the sequel to the factorial. The factorial climbs like a staircase, one step for each whole number. But what would the factorial of 2.5 be? Multiplying all the way down to one only works in whole steps. We want a ramp, a smooth curve passing over every step. The trouble is, endless smooth curves pass through the same dots.

The rule a ramp must keep, each step up multiplies by s. And it starts at height one on the first step. Climb the rule from the first step, and the factorial falls out. Notice the shift by one; it will matter later. One integral keeps both rules. Euler's Gamma function. It comes with a partner living between zero and one, the Beta function.

Together they are called the Euler integrals, the Gamma function and the Beta function. Gamma of s is the whole area under this curve, out to infinity. Turn the dial, and the area follows; that area is the function. Cut at one; the danger sits at the two shallow ends, like a pool. Zoom in near zero; there only the power matters. Near zero, the power has finite area exactly when s is positive. Set s to zero and it breaks; the area near the origin is infinite. Far out, the exponential crushes every power, so the tail always converges. So Gamma converges exactly for positive s.

Beta gets the same check at its two ends, zero and one. Near zero it acts like one power, near one like another. So Beta converges exactly when both numbers are positive.

Is Gamma smooth? The tests from last episode answer that. Near zero, the smallest power caps the whole family. Far out, the largest power caps them all. Both caps have finite area, so the M test gives uniform convergence. Every such window works; that is uniform convergence on closed subintervals. Near zero no single cap can work; Gamma itself explodes. Differentiate under the integral sign, and a log factor appears. The log is tamed the same way, again and again, so Gamma is smooth. The uniform convergence from last episode is doing real work here.

3 · Method

Now the big question. Does one step up really multiply by s? Cut it off far out and integrate by parts. Here comes the risky step: the boundary term dies, because the exponential beats every power. Send the cut to infinity, and each step up multiplies by s. Picture a building where each floor is s times the floor below. The recurrence formula, run down to the bottom, gives the factorial. The ground floor: the area under the exponential is exactly one. Quick checks: Gamma of 2 is 1, and Gamma of 6 is 120. The ramp is found, smooth in between, and exact on every step. Careful, Gamma of n is the factorial one step lower.

Now the shape. Equal to one at one and at two. The second derivative is a positive area, so the graph is strictly convex. Equal heights at one and two force a single lowest point between them. It shoots up to infinity at both ends. Read the rule backwards, and Gamma reaches below zero. Repeat, and the continuation skips only zero and the negative integers. Near each one the size blows up, and the sign keeps flipping.

The course text lists three rules: symmetry, recurrence, and the trigonometric form. Mirror the picture, and the two numbers trade places. Integrate by parts, and the second number drops by one. Substitute a squared cosine, and Beta turns into a sine and cosine integral. Every power of sine, integrated over a quarter turn, is Beta in disguise. Check the cube of sine: two thirds, just as before. Remember Wallis's formula? It samples the Beta family at whole numbers.

Integers first. Step down to the bottom, where the area is one over m. A ratio of factorials, which becomes a ratio of Gamma values, one step up. In general, Beta is a ratio of Gamma values, for all positive inputs. Sanity check: both sides give one. Another check: both routes give one twelfth. The course text saves the general proof for chapter 21, with double integrals.

4 · Outlook

Use one: five rounds of integration by parts collapse into a single value. Use two: a polynomial area in one stroke, no expanding. One value is still owed, Gamma at one half, the next episode's target.

Looking ahead, the Gamma distribution's normalizing constant is a Gamma value. The Beta distribution, between zero and one, divides by a Beta value. Every Gamma shaped constant in a statistics formula comes from here. The staircase got its ramp, and the ramp got a partner.
