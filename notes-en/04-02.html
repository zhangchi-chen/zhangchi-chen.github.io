Hello, everyone.Today: arithmetic, composition, and inverses of continuous functions.And why every elementary function is continuous.A student types sin(1 − 0.999²) into a calculator.The answer appears instantly: about 0.001999.Push 0.999 closer to 1, and the output creeps toward 0.So it seems lim(x→1) sin(1 − x²) = sin 0 = 0.

But wait — what gives us the right to plug in x = 1?A limit is about nearby points, not the point itself.Pushing a limit inside a function needs a license.Today we earn that license.

First, the local face of continuity.Near a point of continuity, the function stays bounded.And if f(x₀) > 0, then f stays positive near x₀.Example: cos 0 = 1 > 0.And cos x stays positive on all of (−π/2, π/2).Two one-line facts: continuity is a local license.

Now the bridge from the chapter-3 limit laws.Picture continuity at x₀ as a handshake.You name ε; the function answers with δ.f shakes hands at x₀, and so does g.Two handshakes make a handshake: f ± g and f·g are continuous at x₀.And f/g too, as long as g(x₀) ≠ 0.Sign preservation keeps g nonzero near x₀, so f/g is defined there.

Composition is pipes welded in series.x flows into g; out comes u = g(x).Then u flows into f; out comes f(g(x)).g is continuous at x₀, and f is continuous at g(x₀).Then the welded pipe f∘g is continuous at x₀.No leak at either joint means no leak overall.Inverse functions: reflect across the mirror y = x.

Take f strictly increasing and continuous on [a, b].Its graph has no gaps and never turns back.The mirror image has no gaps either.So f⁻¹ is continuous on [f(a), f(b)].If f is strictly decreasing, the domain of f⁻¹ is [f(b), f(a)].Here are the theorems, stated once.Arithmetic: sums, differences, products, and quotients (g(x₀) ≠ 0) stay continuous.

Composition: g continuous at x₀ and f continuous at g(x₀) give f∘g continuous at x₀.Inverse: f strictly monotone and continuous on [a, b] gives f⁻¹ continuous.Consequently, every elementary function is continuous on its domain.Let's prove one rule honestly: the sum.Fix ε > 0.Continuity of f gives δ₁ > 0 such that, whenever |x − x₀| < δ₁,we get |f(x) − f(x₀)| < ε/2.

Likewise, δ₂ > 0 gives |g(x) − g(x₀)| < ε/2.Take δ = min(δ₁, δ₂).Then the triangle inequality does the rest.The total error is less than ε/2 + ε/2 = ε.Done: f + g is continuous at x₀.The difference f − g: the same argument.The other rules are translations of the chapter-3 limit laws.

Continuity at x₀ means f(x) → f(x₀) as x → x₀.Product law: lim f·g = lim f · lim g = f(x₀)·g(x₀).Quotient law: the same, provided g(x₀) ≠ 0.Composition: ε for f gives η; then η for g gives δ.Inverse: the mirror argument on [a, b]; we take that theorem as given.

Now let's grow the family tree.Five roots: constants, powers xᵅ,trigonometric functions and their inverses,the exponential aˣ, and the logarithm logₐ x.Each root is continuous. For sin, one bound does it:|sin x − sin x₀| ≤ |x − x₀|.For the exponential, recall from chapter 1, for a > 1:

aˣ = sup{aʳ : r ∈ ℚ, r ≤ x}.Its continuity we simply use as known.Three kinds of grafts: arithmetic, composition, inversion.The logarithm is even the mirror image of the exponential.And for x > 0, xᵅ = exp(α ln x) is a composition.Harvest: every elementary function is continuous on its intervals of definition.Example: h(x) = √(4 − x²)/(x − 1).

Its domain is [−2, 1) ∪ (1, 2].And it is continuous at every point there.Use one: limits by substitution.sin and 1 − x² are both continuous,so lim(x→1) sin(1 − x²) = sin 0 = 0, in one line.The calculator was right, and now we know why.The composition footnote: what if the inner function has a hole?

sin x/x is undefined at 0, yet its limit is 1: a removable flaw.If f(x) → A and g is continuous at A,then lim g(f(x)) = g(lim f(x)) = g(A).As x → 0: √(2 − sin x/x) → √(2 − 1) = 1.As x → ∞: sin x/x → 0, so √(2 − sin x/x) → √2.Both are fair game: the outer square root is continuous.Use two: the power limit rule.

If u(x) → a > 0 and v(x) → b, then u(x)^v(x) → a^b.Why? Write u^v = exp(v ln u).ln is continuous at a > 0; exp is continuous at b ln a.So exp(v ln u) → exp(b ln a) = a^b.Example, as x → ∞: the base (2x + 1)/(x + 5) → 2,and the exponent 3x/(x + 1) → 3.So ((2x + 1)/(x + 5))^(3x/(x + 1)) → 2³ = 8.

Note: we need a > 0 and a finite b.Use three: the arcsin family.sin is strictly increasing and continuous on [−π/2, π/2].By the inverse rule, arcsin is continuous on [−1, 1].cos is strictly decreasing on [0, π], so arccos is continuous on [−1, 1].tan is strictly increasing on each [−c, c] with 0 < c < π/2;so arctan is continuous on each [−tan c, tan c], and these cover ℝ.

Example: lim(x→2) arcsin(x/4) = arcsin(1/2) = π/6.One honest warning label on this license.The license is void outside the domain.√x near x = −1? It is not even defined there.Also where denominators vanish: sin x/x at x = 0 gives 0/0.Substitution says nothing there; that limit needs chapter-3 tools.Read the label before you plug in: domain and denominators.
