Motivation

  1. Parametric curves: dy/dx is a ratio of two rates.
  2. Your friend's dot is moving across the campus map.
  3. The app knows its eastward speed...
  4. ...and, separately, its northward speed.
  5. But your eye wants one thing: where does the arrow point?
  6. That direction is a slope: northward change over eastward change.
  7. Here: 2 east, 1 north, so the slope is 1/2.
  8. Now the homework version: x = t + eᵗ, y = sin t.
  9. Find the slope of the curve at t = 0.
  10. The reflex: solve for t from x, then plug into y.
  11. But t can't be written as an elementary function of x.
  12. The tangent is clearly there; we just can't reach it.
  13. Another trick: cancel the dt. Fast, but is that a reason?
  14. Today: the honest reason this ratio of rates works.

What the statement says

  1. The setup, in the course text's words.
  2. A point moves; at time t it sits at (φ(t), ψ(t)).
  3. t is the parameter: a clock, not a coordinate.
  4. Nudge the clock by Δt; the point jumps to a nearby spot.
  5. Join the two spots: that's a secant.
  6. Its slope: change in ψ over change in φ.
  7. Divide top and bottom by Δt: fair, both are real numbers.
  8. Let Δt → 0: top and bottom tend to the two derivatives.
  9. The bottom's limit isn't zero, so the secant slope → ψ′(t₀)/φ′(t₀).
  10. So: tangent slope = vertical speed ÷ horizontal speed.
  11. Why demand φ′(t₀) ≠ 0? The bottom must not vanish.
  12. Zero east speed: the arrow points due north, no finite slope.
  13. Formula (2) says the same for every t.
  14. It names its route: y = ψ(φ⁻¹(x)), a function of x through t.
  15. Undo φ to get back t, then feed t to ψ.
  16. Well-behaved curves get a name: smooth curves.
  17. Continuous derivatives: the two speed dials never jump.
  18. φ′² + ψ′² ≠ 0: the two speeds are never both zero.
  19. The point never stops, so it always has a heading.
  20. Hence a tangent everywhere, its angle α(t) turning continuously.
  21. A counterexample: x = t³, y = |t|³.
  22. Both have continuous derivatives, yet the curve is a V.
  23. At t = 0 both speeds are zero: the point stops at the corner.
  24. Stopped, it can turn a sharp corner; moving, it can't.

Method / proof core

  1. The real reason uses two tools from 05-04.
  2. Step one: y is a function of x through t.
  3. Chain rule: dy/dx = dy/dt · dt/dx.
  4. But where does dt/dx come from? t was never written in x.
  5. Inverse rule: dt/dx = 1/(dx/dt), just flip it.
  6. Flipping needs dx/dt ≠ 0, the very same condition.
  7. Mesh the two gears, and out comes ψ′(t)/φ′(t).
  8. Every step was a theorem from 05-04; nothing was cancelled.
  9. So 'cancel the dt' is a memory aid, not a reason.
  10. For now dt isn't a number; 05-08 will give it that meaning.
  11. Course-text example: the upper half ellipse.
  12. First the two rates: −a sin t and b cos t.
  13. Divide: dy/dx = −(b/a) cot t.
  14. At t = π/2 the slope is 0: flat at the top.
  15. As t → 0⁺ the slope → −∞: the tangent turns vertical.
  16. Just like the ellipse: flat on top, steep at the ends.
  17. At the end itself dx/dt = 0, so the formula is off duty.
  18. Yet a tangent exists: dy/dt ≠ 0 there, so it is vertical.
  19. With a = 2, b = 1, at t = π/4 the slope is −1/2.
  20. Point plus slope gives the tangent: y = √2 − x/2.
  21. Next, the cycloid: the path of a pebble stuck in a bike tyre.
  22. Its parametric equations, with wheel radius a.
  23. Again, first the two rates.
  24. Divide and simplify: the slope is cot(t/2).
  25. At t = π/2 the slope is 1: climbing at 45°.
  26. At t = π the slope is 0: the top of the arch.
  27. At t = 2π both rates are 0: the pebble touches the road and stops.
  28. There the curve has a cusp: exactly the stop we warned about.
  29. The heading flips: straight down coming in, straight up going out.
  30. Polar curves too: x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, with θ as the clock.
  31. Named once: the cardioid, r = a(1 + cos θ).
  32. The course text's tan φ = r(θ)/r′(θ) gives the tangent–radius angle.
  33. One last temptation: the second derivative.
  34. The guess: d²y/dx² = ψ″(t)/φ″(t), the same pattern again?
  35. Test it on the cycloid at t = π/2, with a = 1.
  36. Classmate A follows the guess: cos t / sin t gives 0.
  37. Classmate B differentiates the slope, then divides by dx/dt: −1.
  38. First readouts agree; second readouts clash: 0 versus −1.
  39. The first formula was earned by two rules; the guess was only copied.
  40. Why the pattern breaks is 05-09's business, not today's.

Applications and outlook

  1. Back to the homework, at t = 0.
  2. The rates: dx/dt = 2 and dy/dt = 1.
  3. Slope 1/2, tangent y = (x − 1)/2, no need to solve for t.
  4. Same 2 east, 1 north as your friend's dot on the map.
  5. Use one: a robot arm's tip, logged as (x(t), y(t)).
  6. Its heading is y′(t)/x′(t), straight from today's formula.
  7. Use two: a basketball shot, x = 3t, y = 4t − 5t².
  8. Slope (4 − 10t)/3 hits 0 at t = 0.4: the top of the arc.
  9. Looking back: 05-04's chain and inverse rules, working as a pair.
  10. Looking ahead: 05-08 makes this division mean exactly what it says.
  11. And 05-09 settles the puzzle of 0 versus −1.
  12. Wherever motion is logged by time, (x(t), y(t)) is the natural format.
  13. The ratio of the two rates is the direction you're actually heading.
  14. A ratio of two rates: the arrow on the map.
