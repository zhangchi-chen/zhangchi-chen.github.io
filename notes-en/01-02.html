Real Analysis · Episode 1.2

Density: rationals and irrationals everywhere

Between any two real numbers, however close, there are infinitely many rationals and infinitely many irrationals. Yet neither family fills the line.

0The statement

Definition

A set S of real numbers is dense in ℝ if every open interval (a, b), however small, contains a point of S.

Theorem

Both ℚ and its complement ℝ∖ℚ are dense in ℝ.

Density does not say S = ℝ, and it does not say S has no gaps. It only says: no window, however thin, avoids S.

1Two pictures

Rain

Drops are the points of S; a patch of ground is an open interval. Dense rain hits every patch, however small.

A comb refined without end

The teeth (rationals) reach into every segment of the line. Zoom in, and every segment still has gaps (irrationals). Teeth and gaps both appear in every segment.

2Why a rational always lands inside

  1. Given a < b, choose n ∈ ℕ with 1/n < b − a. (Such n exists: the Archimedean property.)
  2. Ruler marks k/n are spaced 1/n apart, closer than the window is wide, so they cannot all jump over it.
  3. Let m/n be the first mark with m/n > a. Its left neighbour satisfies (m−1)/n ≤ a.
  4. Hence a < m/n ≤ a + 1/n < b, so m/n ∈ ℚ lies in (a, b).

Decimal rulers: marks every 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, … — however small the window, some ruler drops a finite decimal (a rational) inside. Repeating in smaller windows gives infinitely many.

3Irrationals are dense too

  1. Given (a, b), look at the scaled window (a/√2, b/√2).
  2. Pick a rational r ≠ 0 inside it (there are infinitely many, so avoiding 0 is easy).
  3. Stretch the line by √2: then √2·r ∈ (a, b).
  4. If √2·r = q ∈ ℚ, then √2 = q/r ∈ ℚ, a contradiction. So √2·r ∉ ℚ.

4The trap: dense ≠ complete

ℚ is dense, yet x² = 2 has no rational solution: rational marks crowd every window around √2, but none sits at √2. Dense rain can still miss a chosen spot.

Density

every window gets hit

Completeness

every convergent hunt finds its target

These are different promises. The next episode is about the second one.

5Recap

  • Both ℚ and ℝ∖ℚ are dense in ℝ.
  • Between any two reals lie infinitely many rationals and infinitely many irrationals.
  • None of this fills the line by itself: dense ≠ complete.
